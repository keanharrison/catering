## Overall Impression

A composed, credible start to a complex request flow: the steps and order summary make the task legible. The biggest opportunity is to make the customer surface feel trustworthy and self-explanatory at the moment of menu choice, while keeping walkthrough controls in their own context.

## What’s Working

- Five visible steps make a complex request feel finite.
- Persistent order summary ties item choices to guest count, estimate, and serving coverage.
- Review can be edited and clearly says submission is local demo state, not a real order or payment.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Separate demo controls from the customer experience.** Customer/Staff switching, scenario selection, and Reset share the shell with the request journey. A first-time customer can mistake demo controls for part of a real service. Put walkthrough controls behind a dedicated demo mode or make their status unmistakable. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`.
2. **[P1] Clarify dietary tags at the point of choice.** “Gluten-free” and “Dairy-free” can be read as allergy or ingredient guarantees, while the product record says they are not. Add a concise clarification next to the tags before users rely on them. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`.
3. **[P2] Resolve the disabled assistant affordance.** The panel shows sample questions and an input that are all disabled. It invites interaction and can look broken. Collapse it or make its unavailable state visually unmistakable. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill`.
4. **[P2] Explain serving assumptions where quantities are chosen.** Coverage count is useful, but customers may not know how one serving maps to a meal or how shared sides count. Put the assumption beside the quantity controls and qualify recommendation logic. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`.
5. **[P2] Clarify the simulated next step after submission.** The review correctly says no order is sent; give a concrete expectation for what happens in this prototype so the ending feels complete. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`.

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan, first-timer:** Could mistake dietary labels for safety promises or not know how much food to order. The disabled assistant adds uncertainty at the point questions arise.
- **Sam, accessibility-dependent:** Labeled standard controls and a skip link help. The long menu creates a lengthy linear screen-reader/keyboard journey, and disabled assistant controls have no apparent reason in the interaction.
- **Casey, distracted mobile user:** The multi-step flow and long menu are interruption-prone. A fixed continue action helps, but no save-and-resume cue reassures someone who leaves mid-request.

## Minor Observations

- The logo reads small beside the expansive header and oversized page titles.
- Prefilled scenario values may be mistaken for customer-entered data if scenario controls are overlooked.
- The menu’s no-match empty state is clear; a one-tap clear-filters action would speed recovery.
- Review’s nearby Edit links keep corrections close to the relevant fields.

## Questions to Consider

- If recommendation rules are hypotheses, should assumptions be shown before the customer applies a recommendation?
- Which walkthrough controls need to be visible during stakeholder demos, and which should be removed from the customer’s mental model?
- What would make a request feel complete when this prototype deliberately sends nothing?
