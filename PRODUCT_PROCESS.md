# Product Build Guidance

This project should follow John Zettler’s product management process.

## Core PM Responsibilities

The PM owns the **what** and the **why**.

Engineering and design own the **how** and the **when**.

The PM must:

- Create value for customers.
- Grow business impact.
- Assemble and align the team.
- Be the voice of the customer.
- Take accountability for outcomes.
- Lead through influence, not authority.
- Be first to take accountability and last to take credit.

## Product Sense

Before building, clarify what problem is worth solving.

Use two inputs:

1. Customer conversations
2. Data analysis

For this project, customer conversations include Sober, restaurant staff, catering customers, and prospective restaurant owners.

Data analysis can include sample orders, menu rules, pricing, calendars, customer questions, and competitor workflows.

Stay customer-obsessed, not competitor-obsessed.

## Strategy Before PRD

Product strategy comes before the PRD.

Before coding, define:

- End-state vision
- Target user
- Customer problem
- Why this solution should work
- What v1 must prove
- What later versions can add

The PRD should describe what we will build and why it matters.

## Roadmap

Maintain a simple ranked roadmap.

Each item should include:

- Priority: P0, P1, P2, or P3
- Expected impact
- Build complexity
- Current status
- Reason it matters

Keep the roadmap in priority order.

Say no to anything that does not support the current goal.

## Product Execution Lifecycle

Follow this order:

1. Research
2. Plan
3. Design
4. Implement
5. Test
6. Release
7. Measure

Do not skip steps.

## PRD And Kickoff

Before implementation:

- Write the PRD.
- Identify open questions and assumptions.
- Define success metrics.
- Define guardrail metrics.
- Review the scope.
- Lock the initial v1 scope.

## Design And Technical Planning

After PRD:

- Design creates mockups.
- Engineering drafts a technical design.
- PM revises the PRD based on design and technical constraints.
- Finalize the build scope before implementation.

## Implementation

During implementation:

- Build only what supports the locked v1 scope.
- Keep decisions and tradeoffs written down.
- Preserve the customer problem as the center of the work.
- Avoid generic dashboard features unless they directly support the catering workflow.

## Testing And Dogfooding

Every teammate is responsible for testing.

Test:

- Customer flow
- Owner/admin flow
- Edge cases
- Empty states
- Error states
- Mobile layout
- Demo narrative
- Whether the product clearly communicates value

## Launch

For this project, launch means producing a polished client demo that can be shown to Sober and other prospective restaurant clients.

The demo must make the before/after workflow obvious.

## Measurement

Every launch needs success and guardrail metrics.

Success metrics show whether the product achieved the intended effect.

Guardrail metrics show whether it caused unintended problems.

For this project, measure:

- Can a user understand the product quickly?
- Does the guided ordering flow feel useful?
- Does the owner review flow preserve human judgment?
- Does the demo feel restaurant-specific rather than generic?
- Does it create useful feedback from prospective users?

## Communication Rules

Write briefly and clearly.

For any document longer than two pages, start with an executive summary.

Write down:

- Decisions
- Action items
- Open questions
- Tradeoffs
- Agreements

Documents are durable artifacts and should be treated as part of the product.

## Collaboration Rules

Assume teammates want the same outcome but see the problem through different lenses.

When disagreeing:

- Listen first.
- Restate the other side’s strongest argument.
- Write both points of view clearly.
- Escalate only when blocked.
- Disagree and commit once a decision is made.

## AI Usage Rules

AI can assist thinking, but cannot replace understanding.

Use AI to:

- Research
- Explore
- Challenge
- Improve
- Review
- Test

Own every word of AI-generated output.

Do not accept vague, generic, or unverified work.
